# Juris C-75A: faceted acoustic archtop on the C-75 outline

Parametric model. Units are mm. Axes: x+ toward the treble side, y+ toward the headstock (y = 0 at the tail), z+ up (z = 0 at the back edge).

## Files
- `juris_c75a.glb`: full assembly (open it in Blender, Fusion or any glTF viewer)
- `top.stl`, `back.stl`, `rim.stl`, `neck.stl`, `fingerboard.stl`, `bridge.stl`: watertight solids for CAM
- `juris_c75a_templates.dxf`: 2D templates on separate layers. OUTLINE, RIM_INNER (8 mm wall), LINING_INNER (12 mm ledge for the back), TOP_PLATEAU, TOP_BEAM, TOP_RIB, TOP_FOLDS, SOUNDHOLES, SOUNDHOLE_LIPS, BACK_PLATEAU, BACK_FOLDS, CENTERLINE, BRIDGE_LINE
- `report.json`: derived numbers (bridge height, clearances, weight estimate, air volume)
- `fea.py` and `fea.json`: static and modal finite-element check of the top under string load (quadratic tets, orthotropic quartersawn Port Orford cedar, top fixed where it is glued to the rim and neck block). Run it with `FEA_WOOD=spruce` to compare against Sitka. On the same mesh, POC sinks 8% less at the bridge (0.51 vs 0.56 mm), has 29% more margin along the grain (safety factor 8.5 vs 6.6), and its first top modes sit 2–3% higher (418/527/833 Hz vs 406/512/823 Hz, with a rigid rim). Cross-grain margin is unchanged at 4.3×, because POC's cross-grain strength isn't published, so the check assumes spruce's.
- `crease2d.py` and `crease2d.json`: a plane-strain section across one fold of the 3 mm top (cross-grain, orthotropic), sharp vs. radiused on the re-entrant side, under bending. A sharp crease concentrates cross-grain stress 2.1× (8° fold) to 5× (22° fold) over the plain plate, and that figure keeps rising as the mesh gets finer, because a sharp inside corner is a true stress singularity. The 25 mm radius caps it at about 1.4× for every fold angle in the top. The 3D FEA mesh is too coarse to resolve this, which is why it's checked in section.
- `build.py` and `outline.py`: the generator. Edit `P` in build.py and run `python build.py` (needs manifold3d, trimesh, mapbox_earcut, ezdxf, numpy)

## How the geometry is built
- **Outline:** traced from Juris_C_75_Body_2D.pdf and calibrated on its 18.000 and 13.500 dimensions. The lower bout is symmetrised, and the corners are filleted (12 to 19 mm, sized for a 12 mm end mill).
- **Top:** made of planes only, symmetric about the centreline. A flat hexagonal bridge plateau (141 x 155 mm, wide enough for the 135 mm Gretsch base) sits 11 mm above the edge inside an eleven-sided arch zone that sits inside the glue ledges and carries on into both upper bouts (symmetric through the lower bout and waist, following the outline above it, round the cutaway on the treble side); 17 triangular facets run from the plateau to that octagon (every fold has a 25 mm radius blended into its inside, the re-entrant side, so the creases aren't knife lines; the outside of each ridge stays sharp), and outside it the top is a flat skirt at edge level that sits on the rim and glue ledges. Falls taper 2.8 mm at the plateau to 3.2 mm at the skirt, the plateau is 3.5 mm thick, a 5 x 6 mm ring beam runs under the plateau edge (the base's feet land on it), and two 6 x 5 mm tone bars with 35 mm scalloped ends run lengthwise under the plateau's long sides, and a 6 x 5 mm cross bar runs from the neck block's lower face out to the bass upper bout, tapering over its last 35 mm and stopping 2 mm short of the glue ledge (all on DXF layer TOP_BARS). The two soundholes are mirror images in the side facets beside the bridge (about 18 x 35 mm each), outboard of the bars. A 4 mm wide, 7.5 mm deep lip hangs under each one and joins the ring beam; it lengthens the port so the air resonance sits near 127 Hz (between B2 and C3, clear of E3) without a deeper rim. The pickup lead drops through a 6 mm hole under the fingerboard extension.
- **Back:** quartersawn bigleaf maple (top is quartersawn Port Orford cedar). A broad arch 22 mm deep (raised from 12 mm to win back air with the 1-3/4" rim) over the same eleven-sided footprint as the top's arch zone, rising to a centre ridge 190 mm long: 13 flat triangular facets, and a flat border outside it that sits on the glue ledge. 3.2 mm thick throughout, unbraced; the ridge and footprint fold lines act as stiffeners (DXF layer BACK_FOLDS).
- **Rim:** one ring cut from a standard 1-3/4" mahogany body blank, all from the top face in one setup, for a 2.0" body at the edge (the same as the C-75). It has 8 mm walls (the top glues to the full wall), a 12 x 8 mm glue ledge at the bottom for the back, solid neck and tail blocks, a neck mortise, the pot window and battery-box opening, and the jack hole.
- **Edges:** 3.5 mm 45-degree chamfer around the top and back edges, cut after glue-up through the plate and into the rim.
- **Heel and volute:** the '59 round carve runs all the way into the heel, which sweeps down to finish flush with the back edge on the body face. The heel is 53 mm wide, 1.5 mm inside the body's sides, and lands on a square, unchamfered end face. Behind the nut the round neck section morphs into the square headstock section with no step; the volute loft forms the headstock itself up to the first tuners, so there is no seam at the base. The edge chamfer fades out over the last 28 mm before the neck end face.
- **Neck tongue:** the neck continues as a tongue under the fingerboard extension, filling the space between the top and the board over the neck block (to y = 392 mm) and glued to both; the board overhangs past it toward the pickup. The tenon passes down through a notch in the top, so the neck is set from above after the body is closed.
- **Neck:** set neck at 5 degrees with a mortise and tenon. The tenon sits below the top, and the neck clears the body at about fret 15. '59 roundback profile, 12" radius, 22 frets. 13-degree 3x3 Juris headstock, the same shape as the built C-75: waisted sides, a pointed gable top, tuners in near-straight columns, flat face with square edges for standard tuners, maple headstock face (finished to match the top), Richlite fingerboard, pointed nickel truss rod cover, posts in near-straight columns. A lofted volute (about 4.5 mm proud) blends the neck into the headstock behind the nut.
- **Electronics:** no pickguard. An EMG 91 active floating humbucker hangs from a strap under the fingerboard extension; its lead goes into the body through a 6 mm hole in the top hidden under the board. Everything else is a stock part. The flat treble lower-bout face of the rim carries a 36 × 36 × 2 mm plate for the stacked volume/tone pot (over a 26 mm round window) and, beside it toward the tail, a Gotoh BB-04 9 V battery box (StewMac #3577 is the same format) in a 57 × 25 mm opening. The hinged lid makes battery changes tool-free. The stereo output jack is a Switchcraft 152B in a 12 mm hole in the flat treble tail facet. Both control-face openings and the jack hole are routed or drilled from outside after the rim is machined (`rim_control_face_template.dxf`). The wall behind them is left at the full 20 mm ledge thickness for the screws, and the active pickup needs no string ground.
- **Optional pickup:** a Curtis Novak DeArmond-style gold foil in the mustache casing, on a bent strap from the fingerboard end with a shelf under the casing's ears (spring height adjustment). Placeholder sizes in `GOLDFOIL`; passive, so 500k pots and a string ground. Flat pattern in `cnc/flat/goldfoil_bracket_flat.dxf`.
